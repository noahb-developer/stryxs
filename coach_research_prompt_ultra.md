## ROUND 1 (new conversation)

You are a world-class ultrarunning coach and exercise physiologist specializing in trail, mountain, and road ultras for adult amateur through competitive runners. The marathon factor tables must NOT leak into this persona, and vice versa. Cover the spectrum and mark where they diverge:
- [50K] the entry ultra, closest to a marathon.
- [50MI / 100K] the mid ultras, where fueling and durability start to dominate.
- [100MI+] the long ultras, adding night running, sleep management, deep pacing/crew strategy.
- Terrain modifiers throughout: [TRAIL/MOUNTAIN] (significant vertical gain + technical footing) vs [ROAD/FLAT] (road ultras, track, flat 50k/100k) vs [TIMED/BACKYARD] (6/12/24 h, last-person-standing, multi-day stage races).

Be specific and quantitative: numbers, ranges, formulas, tables — not vague philosophy. Ground it in and cite the established methodologies (Jason Koop — Training Essentials for Ultrarunning; Krissy Moehl — Running Your First Ultra; Hal Koerner; David Roche; Steve House & Scott Johnston — Training for the Uphill Athlete; Phil Maffetone — MAF aerobic method; Stephen Seiler — polarized; Joe Friel; Magness & Marcora — effort/brain; UESCA ultrarunning; Western States / UTMB practice; Tim Noakes). Reminder of the athlete data the app has, so every rule keys off real fields: run PBs (5K / 10K / half / marathon / last race), run LTHR, recent runs with distance / duration / average pace / average HR / elevation gain (vert) / HR drift %, longest recent run, experience tier, available training days, weekly hours or weekly km, weekly vertical gain if logged, equipment (trail access, treadmill with incline, poles, hydration vest, gym, GPS watch), age / sex, injuries, and a live training-load model (CTL fitness, ATL fatigue, TSB form, ACWR), plus completed-vs-planned sessions.

3. ZONES & EFFORT — the run zone model by % LTHR (and by RPE), with each zone's purpose, and the special ultra emphasis on Z1-Z2 aerobic. - TERRAIN-TECHNICALITY and SURFACE factor (smooth trail vs technical rock/root vs road) as a pace multiplier.
- AID-STATION / STOPPAGE time: a rule for total non-moving time by distance and runner level.
- [100MI+] NIGHT / SLEEP-DEPRIVATION slowdown factor.
- DEFAULT terrain, vert, and pace when the course profile or the runner's data is unknown; and a default fitness when only limited data exists.
- The MINIMUM data for a credible projection; concrete LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH confidence criteria (data thresholds) — note ultra confidence is inherently wider than road; the +/- % range per level.
- A FINISH-PROBABILITY notion for first-timers (is their training enough to finish inside the cutoff), separate from a time prediction.
- WORKED EXAMPLE, step by step: an intermediate runner, 3:30 marathon PB, training ~7 h/wk with ~600 m vert/wk, longest run 4 h — project a 50K with 1500 m vert on smooth trail AND a 100K with 3500 m vert on technical trail: show the flat-equivalent pace, the vert add, the terrain factor, the aid-station time, the finish time, the confidence label, and the range. Keep every number internally consistent — I will unit-test the code against them.

9. ANTI-PATTERNS / SAFETY RULES — explicit DO-NOT rules with numbers, ultra-specific: ramping time-on-feet or weekly vert too fast (the back-to-back-block injury), all flat training for a mountain race (untrained descending = quad blowup), neglecting downhill/eccentric work, never practicing fueling (race-day GI disaster), too much intensity / not enough aerobic base, ignoring sleep and life stress, chronic underfueling / RED-S risk in high-volume blocks, heat/altitude naivety. Include load-spike / ACWR / no-deload guardrails AND a separate vertical-load guardrail.

10. FEASIBILITY / SANITY CHECK — given a goal (finish or a target time at a distance + terrain/vert) + weeks-to-event + current fitness, judge whether it is realistic and the minimum training (weekly hours, longest run in time, weekly vert, back-to-back block, weeks) it requires; how a first-timer's finish-readiness is assessed (longest run as a fraction of race time, total weekly hours, vert preparedness vs course vert). How fast ultra-specific fitness (aerobic durability, vert capacity, descending durability) realistically improves vs road speed. Pair any "unrealistic" verdict with the specific lever (more weeks, more time-on-feet, more vert, more downhill work, fueling practice, or a softer goal/distance).

FORMAT: clear headers and bullets, tables for anything numeric (phase durations, hours/vert->runs maps, zone %LTHR, ultra slowdown by distance, vert-add per 1000 m, terrain factors, aid-station time, confidence thresholds), explicit numbers and rules I can turn into code, mark every [50K vs 50MI/100K vs 100MI+] and [TRAIL/MOUNTAIN vs ROAD/FLAT vs TIMED/BACKYARD] divergence, every rule keyed to beginner/intermediate/advanced WITH a default, thorough but dense, no filler.
